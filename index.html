eBay Fees in the UK: What a £90 Business Order Really Costs

eBay in the UK has no single final value fee for business sellers — the rate depends on the category. That is the first thing to check before you price, because the difference between categories is real money on every order.

For many categories — antiques, art, baby, collectables, dolls, garden, health and beauty, and toys — the final value fee is 10.9% of the total sale. On a £90 order that is £9.81. Add the per-order fee of £0.40 for orders over £10, and the regulatory operating fee of 0.35% (£0.32), and a business seller pays £10.53 on a £90 order, keeping £79.47. Other categories differ: cameras and mobile phones pay 9.9%, jewellery and watches 14.9% up to £1,000, and home, furniture and DIY 11.9% up to £500 — so check your own category in the official table, not a single headline rate.

Private sellers in the UK pay something very different: 0%. UK-based private sellers pay no final value fee and no regulatory operating fee, except on motors listings. That is why so many casual sellers stay private — but a business seller cannot use the private rate, and the moment selling becomes a business, the category fee applies.

Shopify in the UK is the usual comparison: £25 a month for the Basic plan, plus 2% plus 25p per order in card fees — £2.05 on a £90 order. Against eBay's £10.53, the saving is £8.48 an order, so the £25 subscription is covered at about 3 orders a month. Below that, eBay's per-order cost is cheaper; above it, your own store wins on every sale.

The honest test is per-order, not per-platform.
